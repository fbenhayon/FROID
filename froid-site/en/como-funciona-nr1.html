FROID NR-1 / How it works

A management cycle, not just a questionnaire.

The corporate product organizes information about working conditions. It does not use clinical voice and face analysis to classify workers.

Prepare the campaign scope

1. Define units and responsibilities

Describe the structure, activities, units, departments and headcount. Name those responsible for the project and for the technical assessment. Decide which breakdowns would be useful, without presuming that all of them can be disclosed.

2. Prepare purpose, communication and support

Document the purpose of the campaign, the conditions of participation and the support channel for workers. Review the criteria and the risk matrix with the technical managers. Transparency must come before collection.

3. Collect and gather evidence

The questionnaire addresses working conditions. It must be contextualized with observation of the actual activity, dialogue, document analysis and other relevant sources. A survey on its own does not replace the whole assessment.

4. Examine eligible results

Access to aggregated results depends on the rules for closing, participation and protection of groups. “It was not possible to conclude” is different from “there is no risk”. Insufficient data must be recorded.

Understand disclosure and privacy

5. Organize documentation and action

Gather the characterization of the work, hazards, exposed groups, criteria and assessment. The plan must identify what will be done, by whom, by when and how it will be followed up. The inventory and the AEP (ergonomic work analysis) documentation must reflect the technical assessment, not just the output of a form.

6. Implement and follow up

The organization carries out the measures and records evidence. Like a logbook, the history must make it possible to understand decisions and changes — not merely display the final result.

7. Reassess and review

Comparisons between moments use consistent criteria and take uncertainty into account. An inconclusive result does not prove the absence of an effect; an observed difference, by itself, does not prove causality. The technical manager evaluates the need to correct the measures.

Why nobody can know who answered what

This is the first question any CIPA (workplace accident prevention committee), any union and any worker about to report harassment will ask. The answer is not a promise of conduct — it is a property of the architecture, and it fits into four verifiable facts.

1. The knowledge is born split between two hands

To dispatch the invitations, your company receives the pairing between payroll number and link. FROID does not keep that pairing. On our side there is only a pseudonym, derived from the payroll number by HMAC-SHA256 with a key held on the server — without it, nobody can rebuild the table from a payroll list they already hold. Alone, neither party can link person to answer.

2. The application cannot read an answer

The answer tables carry no read policy, and the database role the application uses has had every privilege over them revoked. It can write an answer and it can request an aggregate — it cannot read one back. The only way out is a function that sums the results and applies the cohort floor inside the SQL itself, so the guarantee survives even a programming error in the layer above.

3. When collection closes, the link is erased

During collection a link exists between the invitation and the answer — it is what prevents the same link from being used twice. The moment the campaign is closed, and in the same commit, that link is erased from the database. From then on the data that would allow the pairing ceases to exist: there is nothing to recover, not even under a court order, because there is nothing to hand over. Every closing records in the audit trail how many links were severed.

4. The result only appears aggregated

No breakdown is published below the cohort floor, and insufficient breakdowns are declared insufficient rather than disappearing from the screen — suppressing hides, declaring documents. Results are also not released during collection, because the difference between two moments would reveal the answer of whoever came in between.

What we do not promise. Your company distributes the links and therefore knows whether you answered — never what you answered. We say so plainly in the invitation header, and not as a formality: promising anonymity of participation as well would be the one overstatement capable of destroying the credibility of everything we do guarantee.

The MTE 2025 Guide listing

The Guide to information on Work-Related Psychosocial Risk Factors provides this listing of hazards and their possible consequences. It is indicative and not exhaustive: inspection assesses the coherence of the identification process, not literal adherence to the list. Your company may add hazards specific to its sector and set aside those that do not apply, provided it documents the technical justification.
