Glossary

FROID's terms, in plain language

A quick reference for clinicians and the technically curious. Where a term is established science, we say so; where it's FROID's proprietary framework, we say that too.

Proprietary index
IPM — Multimodal Power Index
The "speedometer" of session energy: quantifies, from 0 to 100, the patient's expressive intensity and psychomotor vitality, fusing voice, face, and semantics. A sustained drop suggests lethargy; spikes suggest hyperactivation.
Proprietary index
IDM — Multimodal Deviance Index
The "compass" of coherence: assesses whether the patient's reactions are congruent with the content. Points out incongruities between speech and facial expression (dissonance), signaling emotional "hot spots."
Acoustics · science
MFCC — Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients
A mathematical representation of vocal timbre, widely used in speech processing. FROID tracks MFCC7 and MFCC9 and their variation over time.
Acoustics · science
MFCC7 / MFCC9
Specific coefficients that, in the anchor study (Zhao et al., 2022), were associated with depression (MFCC7) and somatic anxiety (MFCC9).
Acoustics · science
Δ and ΔΔ (delta and delta-delta)
First and second time derivatives of a coefficient — the "speed" and "acceleration" of change. ΔΔMFCC9 is read as the acceleration of laryngeal tension.
Acoustics · science
F0 — Fundamental Frequency
The pitch of the voice. Lower F0 variability (monotone speech) is a studied marker of psychomotor retardation.
Acoustics · science
ZCR — Zero-Crossing Rate
How many times the audio signal crosses zero per unit of time; a simple indicator of frequency content and noise.
Acoustics · internal proxy
Jitter / Shimmer (proxy)
Frequency instability (jitter) and amplitude instability (shimmer) of the voice. In FROID these are normalized proxy indices, not the classic clinical Praat values.
Acoustics · science
HNR — Harmonic-to-Noise Ratio
Measures how much of the voice is harmonic sound versus noise. Used as a Quality Gate: low HNR indicates degraded audio and reduces the vocal channel's reliability.
Face · science
FACS — Facial Action Coding System
The gold-standard system for describing facial movements in objective units (Action Units), independent of emotional interpretation.
Face · science
AU — Action Unit
Each facial muscle or muscle group coded by FACS. E.g., AU6 (contraction around the eyes) and AU12 (raising of the mouth corners).
Face · science
Onset · Apex · Offset
The three temporal phases of an expression: start, peak, and end. Tracking them helps distinguish spontaneous expressions from voluntary ones. FROID does not track them: each facial reading is evaluated on its own. See what the facial maps do not claim.
Signal · science
FFT — Fast Fourier Transform
An algorithm that decomposes sound into its component frequencies. It is the physical basis behind the spectral energy used in the 12 Zones.
FROID method
Dynamic Baseline (60s calibration)
The patient's "biological fingerprint," measured in the first minute of each session. Every subsequent reading is a deviation relative to it — the main bias-mitigation mechanism.
FROID method
Clinical Screen Stabilization
A proprietary module that separates processing from presentation: FROID keeps processing at a 1-second cadence, but the screen shows a configurable clinical window (real time, 1, 3, 5, or 7 min; default 5 min) so the professional has time to assess all metrics. Critical alerts break the stabilization and update immediately.
FROID method
Moving clinical window / Technical micro-window
Every 1 minute, FROID closes a technical micro-window; the screen consolidates the last 5 by temporal weighted average (10% · 15% · 20% · 25% · 30%, from the oldest to the current minute) — visual stability without losing the gradual reaction to the present.
FROID method
Peak lock
A consolidation rule for the measured signal patterns within the stabilized window: a recent peak does not immediately disappear from the screen, even if the weighted average has already dropped.
FROID method
D_energy — Relative Energy Deviation
How far a zone's current energy deviates from the patient's own baseline. Ensures that stable biological traits are not read as symptoms.
FROID method
M_fac — Facial Dissonance Multiplier
A factor (up to 2.5) that amplifies the IDM when the face contradicts the voice — for example, a voluntary smile (AU12 without AU6) during vocally tense content.
Proprietary framework
12 Perception Zones
A visual interface that distributes vocal energy across 12 bands associated with clinical themes. The physics is real; the theme↔band mapping is a product heuristic, not published neuroscience.
Clinical · FROID
Somatoaffective Dissonance (False Calm)
A state in which stress has been repressed from the face but keeps tensioning the larynx — an apparent calm that the IDM helps reveal.
Architecture
Edge AI (Edge Processing)
Audio and video are processed locally, on the practice's computer, without sending raw biometric files to the cloud.
Privacy
k-anonymity (k ≥ 50)
A technique that guarantees each anonymous record is indistinguishable from at least 49 others, making it infeasible to re-identify a patient in the Data-Froid.
Research
Anonymous Data-Froid
A repository of de-identified session data used to audit bias and statistically validate FROID's heuristics at scale.
