Chain of Command

NR-1

Above all, it is a governance instrument for your company's Chain of Command, in addition to a compliance obligation required by law.

Executive summary

The chain of command is the very organization of work. The legal obligation should be used for a management audit that points directly to where the structure is getting stuck.

A company-wide assessment produces an average, and the average hides the problems.

Campaigns segmented by hierarchical level, branch, store or sector show the differences between units that share the same brand, the same policies and the same product. These differences point to local factors: leadership, workload, communication and processes.

This report recommends three decisions to management:

  1. Treat NR-1 as a governance instrument, in addition to a compliance obligation.
  2. Define the organizational cuts it wishes to compare.
  3. Require the diagnosis to become an action plan in the PGR (Risk Management Program), with periodic reassessment.

Segmented campaigns defined by management

The board chooses the organizational sectors it wishes to compare; FROID runs the same campaign, in the same time window, for all of them. This standardization is what makes the comparison valid.

Your specific needs determine the choice of cuts:

  • ADMINISTRATIVE: board, executive management, departmental directors, management, supervision, staff.
  • GEOGRAPHIC: headquarters, branches, stores, units by city or region.
  • FUNCTIONAL: sectors and departments, such as sales, operations, finance, customer service.
  • CROSS-CUTS: one level within a unit, such as the managers of all stores in the South region.

How a campaign works:

  1. The board defines the objective and the cuts.
  2. FROID checks whether each cut reaches the minimum group of 10 respondents, and the campaign the minimum of 15 completed responses, and proposes aggregations where it does not.
  3. The campaign is run with FROID's own questionnaires, covering the 13 factors in the MTE Guide.
  4. Results are consolidated by cut and cross-referenced with the company's indicators.
  5. The board receives the comparative map with the patterns identified and the action priorities.

Campaigns can be recurring, which makes it possible to measure each unit's progress and assess the effect of the measures adopted.

NR-1 in force: obligation, inspection and legal risk

Since May 26, 2026, the management of psychosocial risks has been enforceable, and since the end of August 2026 labor inspectors can issue citations directly, without the prior advisory double visit.

The standard does not require diagnosing each employee's mental health. The MTE Guide lists 13 factors, among them overload, harassment, low autonomy, lack of leadership support, low organizational justice and poor change management.

What the inspector examines: the risk inventory, an action plan with owner and deadline, evidence of implementation, records of worker participation, and consistency between the documents and reality. The assessment must be carried out at least every two years.

The risk goes beyond fines. In lawsuits over burnout, anxiety or depression, the PGR may be requested as evidence, and the absence of management tends to establish employer fault. Added to this are the social security causal link, an increase in the FAP and recourse actions by the INSS. In 2025, Brazil's Social Security granted 546,254 temporary disability benefits for mental disorders, not all of them work-related.

The hidden opportunity: the chain of command as the object of assessment

Few business owners have realized that NR-1 makes it possible to assess the command structure level by level and unit by unit. The standard lists hierarchical relationships among the work-organization factors; the next step is to compare them.

Vertically, the assessment shows at which level pressure builds up. Horizontally, it shows which unit stands out from its peers under the same command.

Line of reasoning: the four premises

Four premises support the conclusion that segmented assessment locates critical points that a company-wide assessment does not see.

Premise 1: NR-1 looks at the organization, not the individual. Psychosocial risk arises from how work is distributed, demanded, communicated and led. It is the chain of command that defines all of this. Assessing only the company as a whole measures the effect without locating the source.

Premise 2: pressure cascades downward. Each level receives pressure from above and passes it down, amplified or dampened. A level that amplifies it is a bottleneck. The most common case is middle management, held to targets it did not set and responsible for teams it cannot protect.

Premise 3: the average hides the problem. A company with an acceptable overall index may have a branch or a sector in a critical situation, offset by healthy ones. Segmentation shows the variance between units, and it is in the variance that the problem lies.

Premise 4: internal comparison works as a natural experiment. Branches, stores and sectors of the same company share brand, policies, product and pay. When one unit stands out from its peers, the difference tends to stem from local factors: leadership, workload, communication and processes.

Conclusion. If risk is organizational (1), propagates through the hierarchy (2), is diluted in the average (3) and is isolated by comparison among peers (4), then only assessment segmented by level and by unit locates where it arises. The differences found are strong indications, not definitive proof: the diagnosis must be confirmed by qualified listening and by operational indicators.

The five patterns that segmentation reveals

Comparing levels and units produces five patterns, each with a management reading and a question for the board.

Pattern How it shows up in the data What it indicates Question for the board
Vertical gradient Senior management rates the climate much better than the front line Disconnect between those who decide and those who execute Does the strategy reach operations as it was designed?
Bottleneck One specific level concentrates the worst scores Command bottleneck, overload or lack of autonomy Does this level have resources and decision-making power commensurate with what is demanded of it?
Horizontal anomaly A branch, store or sector stands out from its peers under the same command A local management problem What does this unit do differently from the others?
Contagion Sectors that depend on a degraded area also deteriorate Risk propagates through internal interfaces Which internal supplier area is transmitting the problem?
Leadership signature The pattern follows a manager when they move to another unit The factor is the management style, not the place Is the leadership development policy adequate?

The leadership signature pattern requires historical series: it only appears from the second campaign onward, comparing before and after a reassignment. The others already appear in the first campaign.

From climate to results: cross-referencing with indicators

Each unit's psychosocial index gains weight when cross-referenced with indicators the company already has. Where the climate deteriorates and the indicators also deteriorate, the evidence converges.

The MTE itself points in this direction: the questionnaire is not mandatory and, when used, is not sufficient on its own; it must be integrated into the technical analysis. Sick leave, turnover, absenteeism, overtime and complaints often reveal where psychosocial factors are concentrated (Barbieri Advogados).

Indicator group Examples What convergence suggests
People Turnover, absenteeism, INSS sick leave, overtime Direct human cost of the risk
Conduct Reports to the ethics hotline, complaints to the ombudsman, labor lawsuits Legal and reputational exposure
Customer Complaints, NPS, missed deadlines, rework Deterioration in service delivery
Financial Productivity, margin and revenue per unit Impact on operating profitability

Correlation is not causation. The report to the board should present convergences as prioritized indications, with the recommendation to investigate further in the units where psychosocial and operational results deteriorate together.

Anonymity, ethics and LGPD

No result is displayed for cuts with fewer than 10 respondents, nor for campaigns with fewer than 15 completed responses. Below that number, the data is aggregated into the level or unit immediately above.

This rule protects three things at once:

  • The candor of the answers. Without a guarantee of anonymity, employees answer what they think is safe, and the diagnosis loses value.
  • The employee. Segmentation compares groups, never people. A cut that identifies someone could be used for retaliation, which is itself a psychosocial risk factor.
  • The company. Health data is sensitive personal data under the LGPD (Brazil's General Data Protection Law). By receiving only aggregated results, the company reduces its exposure.

In practice, the board and executive management usually have fewer than 10 people. In these cases, we suggest combining them into a single senior-leadership cut or assessing them through qualified listening conducted by an external professional.

The board defines organizational cuts, not targets. This principle should be stated in the engagement agreement and in the communication of the campaign to employees.

From diagnosis to the PGR, and individual care (Psique)

The diagnosis only complies with NR-1 when it becomes a measure recorded in the PGR, with an owner, a deadline and reassessment.

  1. Segmented diagnosis: campaign and cross-referencing with indicators.
  2. Risk inventory: record of the factors identified by unit and level.
  3. Action plan: measures addressing the organizational causes, such as revising targets, team sizing, role clarity and leadership training.
  4. Participation: feedback to employees and consultation on the measures, as required by item 1.5.3.3 of NR-1.
  5. Reassessment: a new campaign to measure the effect and compare before and after.

Individual care, through a separate channel

The organizational assessment identifies where the risk lies. Some employees, however, will need individual assistance. For this, the company's HR psychologist can use FROID Psique, a multimodal clinical analysis platform, to follow these employees and carry out with them a joint assessment of their mental state.

This channel is separate from the organizational campaign and follows four rules:

  • Voluntary: the employee chooses to take part.
  • Consented: specific, free and informed consent, as required by the LGPD for sensitive data.
  • Confidential: the data belongs to the care relationship and remains under the psychologist's professional secrecy.
  • No individual feedback to management: the board, executive management and managers never receive clinical data about individuals. At most, they receive aggregated indicators of service usage.

Recommendations to the board and next steps

We recommend that the board approve the first segmented campaign and treat its results as a governance agenda item.

  • [ ] Appoint a board member or committee responsible for overseeing NR-1.
  • [ ] Define the cuts to compare: levels, branches, stores and sectors.
  • [ ] Authorize access to people, conduct, customer and financial indicators by unit.
  • [ ] Approve the anonymity rule: a minimum of 10 respondents per cut and of 15 completed responses per campaign.
  • [ ] Communicate the campaign to employees, with a commitment to non-retaliation.
  • [ ] Receive the comparative map and approve the action plan in the PGR.
  • [ ] Set the frequency of subsequent campaigns.

Information checklist

Information the company needs to gather before the first conversation, to scope the work, organize data collection and enable a documentable execution.

  • Company structureEstablishments, sectors, activities and intended cuts.
  • HeadcountNumber of workers per unit and per analysis cut.
  • Responsible personsTechnical lead and internal project lead.
  • Worker supportA real, accessible support channel, communicated in advance.
  • CommunicationStatement of purpose, communication plan and participation routine.
  • PGRGrading criteria, matrix and risk levels already used by the company.
  • Existing evidenceDocuments, prevention measures and records already available.
  • Continuity planHow the company intends to act, follow up and reassess results.

How to compare

Five questions for any supplier.

Does the deliverable become an inventory, action plan, AEP (Preliminary Ergonomic Assessment) and criteria?A visual report does not replace traceable documentation.
How is the effectiveness of measures measured?The second assessment is part of the method, not a decorative extra.
Who assesses the effectiveness of the actions carried out?Whoever carries out the action should not be the final assessor of their own service.
How is anonymity applied?Results during collection, or cuts below the floor, can expose who responded.
What happens when a measure does not work?The honest answer is to record the ineffectiveness and correct the cycle.
