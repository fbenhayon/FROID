Management of work-related psychosocial risk factors — NR-1

FROID - Frequency Recognition of Internal Dynamics is a platform developed to support companies in identifying, assessing, documenting, controlling and monitoring work-related psychosocial risk factors.

MTE Ordinance No. 1,419/2024, issued by the Brazilian Ministry of Labor and Employment (MTE), rewrote Chapter 1.5 of NR-1 — Regulatory Standard No. 1, expressly including these factors in GRO (Occupational Risk Management). The obligation is now fully in force.

In practice, this means the company must be prepared not merely to say that it ran a survey, but to demonstrate the entire management process.

What the company must be able to demonstrate

It must be able to answer and document, among other points:

  • which risk factors were identified;
  • at which establishment, address, unit, job role or sector they were found;
  • which workers or groups were potentially exposed;
  • how the actual working conditions were characterized;
  • what evidence was used in the assessment;
  • which methodology and criteria were applied;
  • how the severity and probability of the risk were determined;
  • which classification was assigned to each factor;
  • which prevention measures already existed and whether they were effective;
  • which new measures needed to be introduced, maintained or improved;
  • who was made responsible for each measure;
  • what deadline was set for its implementation;
  • how the execution of the measures was monitored;
  • how their results were measured;
  • whether, after the intervention, the risk actually decreased;
  • and what was done when the measure adopted did not prove effective.

This sequence is not a commercial interpretation by FROID. It follows from the very logic of NR-1 — Regulatory Standard No. 1, which requires hazard identification, risk assessment, prevention measures, follow-up and measurement of results. The PGR (Risk Management Program) must contain, at a minimum, the risk inventory and the action plan, while documenting the criteria makes it possible to show how the classifications used were reached. FROID turns this set of obligations into an organized, traceable and continuously updatable process.

What FROID delivers

FROID does not stop at administering a questionnaire and handing over a slide deck. The platform structures the psychosocial layer of the process and provides the company with the documents and evidence needed to integrate it into its risk management, including:

AEP (Preliminary Ergonomic Assessment)

a document per assessment unit, characterizing the actual working conditions, exposure, the evidence considered and the identified person in charge.

Risk Inventory

with the factors assessed, exposed groups, existing measures and the corresponding risk classification.

Action Plan

with the measures to be introduced, improved or maintained, their owners, schedules, and how they will be monitored and their results measured.

Criteria Document

with the severity and probability gradings, the matrix used, the risk levels and the criteria for classification and decision-making.

Follow-up and effectiveness history

preserving the sequence between what was identified, the action taken and the result subsequently verified.

The difference lies in what happens after the first assessment

The platform was designed to prevent this chain from ending with the first report. FROID allows successive assessment campaigns to be created, targeted at specific establishments, addresses, units and sectors that show risk factors classified as moderate, high or critical, making it possible to verify, after each measure, whether the risk actually decreased. FROID does not carry out these measures: they are implemented by the company itself or by the providers it hires. FROID's role is to assess, independently, whether the actions taken produced the expected result.

Imagine an organization with several units. The first campaign finds that a given factor received a high classification in only two sectors of a particular unit. The company does not need to simply file this information away and wait for a new company-wide assessment: it can act on those factors, record the measures taken and later run a new campaign on exactly that cut.

THE DOCUMENTARY SEQUENCE THAT NOW EXISTS

identification → assessment → classification → prioritization → action → owner → deadline → execution → reassessment → proof of effectiveness or need for correction.

This last point is particularly important. NR-1 requires that, for ergonomic factors — including work-related psychosocial risk factors — the assessment also consider the effectiveness of the prevention measures implemented. And if follow-up shows that a measure is ineffective, it must be corrected.

FROID was developed to document exactly this cycle. Each sector is compared with its own baseline, before and after the measure adopted. The platform preserves the information needed so that the conclusion can be reconstructed and audited later.

For networks with many establishments

Governance Reading: where the risk originates

In companies with many sites, leadership disappears when results are cut by establishment. In an eight-person store, the manager falls below the anonymity minimum and never shows up in the results. And a target set by headquarters shows up as forty store problems, which receive forty local measures that never reach the cause.

Governance Reading assesses each leadership role — managers, assistant managers, supervisors — as one group that spans all sites. Brazil's Ministry of Labour expressly lists “the role” and “the similar exposure group” as assessment units. The risk management programme (PGR) remains per establishment: each role's result is entered in the inventory of every establishment where that role exists.

Anonymity guaranteed by design

Each person answers in a single group, and the anonymity minimums are enforced in the database itself. A role is only read on its own when it has enough people that no individual answer can be inferred.

Linked to the NR-1 inventory

Each role's result becomes a line in the inventory of each establishment, with risk level, exposed group and the corresponding action plan measure.

Reading the chain of command

Shows whether pressure originates in company policy, in the leadership layer or at the site, and marks each action plan measure as corporate, regional or local.

Who it is for. Companies with 20 or more establishments and at least 40 people in the same leadership role. Below that, the role does not have enough people to be read without exposing anyone.

How to contract it. It is an add-on of 30% on the monthly fee, charged only to companies that opt for the cross-site assessment, implemented together with the FROID team. The condition is confirmed with the company's records before the campaign opens, and billing only starts after that confirmation. It is not required to comply with NR-1: assessment by establishment and sector meets the standard on its own.

FROID does not replace the companies that already provide services to your organization

It is important to be clear: FROID does not replace, compete with or seek to take the place of the companies and professionals already hired to provide services related to workers' well-being, health and assistance. These services remain necessary and valuable, among them:

  • social assistance for workers and their families;
  • employee assistance programs (EAP), with psychological, legal, financial and social support;
  • psychological care, therapy, on-call psychological support and listening channels;
  • consulting for implementing actions to improve organizational climate and employees' social well-being;
  • quality-of-life, mental health, mindfulness, workplace exercise and stress prevention programs;
  • leadership training, nonviolent communication, prevention of moral and sexual harassment, and conflict management;
  • diversity, equity and inclusion programs;
  • occupational medicine and safety, SESMT, PCMSO, ergonomics and preparation of the PGR;
  • whistleblowing channels, ombudsman and compliance;
  • climate, engagement and satisfaction surveys;
  • rehabilitation, return-to-work and sick-leave follow-up programs;
  • benefits, population health management and disease prevention programs.

FROID's role is a different one: to give the company the ability to demonstrate, in accordance with NR-1, the processes carried out, the measures adopted and the results actually obtained — including the results of actions conducted by these very providers.

And here lies an essential point: whoever provides a service should never assess the effectiveness of their own service. The law requires that the assessment of psychosocial risk factors and of the effectiveness of prevention measures follow a complex and sophisticated regulatory standard, with methodology, documented criteria, evidence and traceability. An assessment made by whoever carries out the action lacks the independence needed to carry weight before a labor inspection, audit or expert examination.

FROID exists precisely to fill this gap. After all, letting the fox guard the henhouse is a sure way to wake up without a single hen.

Fundamental protection for workers

The company does not receive individual answers. Results are presented in aggregate form and subject to technical criteria of anonymity, representativeness and sufficiency of evidence. During data collection, the company follows participation rates, but does not receive partial results that could allow it to infer what a given person answered.

And when a given sector does not have enough evidence, in quantity or quality, FROID does not turn missing data into a false conclusion of low risk. The sector remains documented: the system records that the evidence was insufficient, states why a given classification could not be produced and indicates what is needed to complete the assessment.

GUIDING PRINCIPLE

Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence of risk.

Why this matters to Management and Legal

In a labor inspection, audit, expert examination or labor lawsuit, the question will rarely be just: “Did the company administer a questionnaire?”. What will need to be demonstrated is the procedure:

  • Did the company know about the risk?
  • How did it become aware of it?
  • How did it assess it?
  • What criterion did it use?
  • What did it decide to do?
  • Who was made responsible?
  • Was the measure actually implemented?
  • And is there contemporaneous evidence that its effectiveness was subsequently verified?

The value of FROID lies precisely in keeping this history organized as it unfolds, rather than trying to reconstruct it years later, when an inspection, expert examination or lawsuit arises.

For this reason, FROID should not be seen merely as a tool for Human Resources or Occupational Health and Safety. It connects Management, the Board, Legal, Compliance, Human Resources and Occupational Health and Safety around a single trail of assessment, decision, action and evidence.

What if the second assessment fails?

A failed result is not a system failure. It is exactly what makes management demonstrable: if the measure did not reduce the risk, the company needs to know it, record it and correct it.

BaselineThe first campaign documents factors, exposed groups, criteria and classification.
MeasureThe company records what it decided to do, who was made responsible and what deadline was set.
ReassessmentThe same cut is assessed again and compared against its own baseline.
No demonstrable improvementThe inventory and the action plan do not pretend success: the measure must be revised, kept under follow-up or replaced.

The cost of inaction

The problem is not just failing to ask.

Did the company know?

What did it decide to do?

Can it reconstruct the history?

Pitfalls

Mistakes that turn an assessment into a liability.

Administering a questionnaire and stopping thereThe questionnaire can be a source, but the obligation is to demonstrate the management process: criterion, classification, measure, owner, deadline and effectiveness.
Mistaking lack of response for low riskAbsence of evidence is not evidence of absence of risk. When the data does not support a conclusion, FROID records insufficiency; it does not invent reassurance.
Letting whoever carries out the action assess itselfSupport programs, consulting, training and climate initiatives remain necessary. The effectiveness of these actions must be verified by an independent method.
Promising confidentiality by contractThe employer must not receive individual answers. Protection has to be built into the architecture and the disclosure floors, not just into a contract clause.
Selling cuts without regard to sizeSmall sectors may require a census or may not release results at all. This must be stated before contracting.

Limits and responsibility

What FROID NR-1/ISO 45003 is, and what it is not.

FROID delivers the instrument, method, evidence and audit trail. The company remains responsible for deciding on, implementing and signing off the prevention measures, with its own professionals and providers.

FROID does not conduct field assessments, does not implement prevention measures, and does not replace SESMT, CIPA, legal counsel, occupational medicine, support programs, psychotherapy, climate consulting or whistleblowing channels.

FROID does not assess individuals. The product deals with working conditions, aggregated results and sufficiency of evidence. It produces no clinical diagnosis, no ranking of workers and no individual decision.
